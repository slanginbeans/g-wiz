G-WIZ for iPad  (version 1.6.1)
===============================

G-WIZ installs on an iPad from a web address. After one visit with internet,
it runs from its own Home Screen icon, full-screen and fully offline. Your
pictures never leave the iPad. The website only hands over the program.

You do steps 1 and 2 once. They take about 10 minutes and are free.


1. Put G-WIZ on a free GitHub Pages website  (on a computer)
------------------------------------------------------------
  a. Go to https://github.com and sign up or sign in.
  b. Click the + at the top right > "New repository".
     Name it  g-wiz  , choose Public, and click "Create repository".
     (Free accounts can only publish public repositories as websites. Anyone
     with the address could open the app, but never your pictures.)
  c. On the new repository page, click "uploading an existing file".
  d. Open the "Upload to GitHub" folder from this zip. Select everything in it
     (index.html, sw.js, manifest.webmanifest, version.txt and the icons
     folder) and drag it all onto the GitHub page. Click "Commit changes".
     The files must sit at the top of the repository, not inside an
     "Upload to GitHub" folder. Check that the icons folder came through
     with its 4 pictures.
  e. Open the repository's Settings > Pages. Under "Build and deployment", set
     Source to "Deploy from a branch". Set Branch to "main" and the folder to
     "/ (root)", then click Save.
  f. Wait a minute or two and refresh the Pages settings. It shows the address:
        https://YOUR-USER-NAME.github.io/g-wiz/


2. Install it on the iPad
-------------------------
  a. On the iPad, open that address in Safari.
  b. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow), then "Add to Home Screen".
     If you see an "Open as Web App" switch, leave it on. Tap Add.
  c. Open G-WIZ from its new Home Screen icon once while you still have
     internet. Wait about 5 seconds so it can store its copy on the iPad.
     (The Home Screen app keeps its own copy, separate from Safari.)
  d. Test it: turn on Airplane Mode, close G-WIZ, and open it again. It works.

  Deleting the icon removes G-WIZ and its settings. To get it back, repeat
  step 2.


3. Updates
----------
  To change the app, replace index.html (or any other file) in the GitHub
  repository: open the file > the pencil icon or "Add file > Upload files" >
  Commit. GitHub publishes the change in about a minute. Its servers may keep
  the old copy for up to 10 minutes.

  The next time you open G-WIZ on the iPad with internet, it compares its copy
  with the website. If something changed, it downloads the new version in the
  background and shows "A new version of G-WIZ has been downloaded".
  Tap "Restart now", or choose Later and the update starts the next time
  G-WIZ opens. Without internet it keeps using the copy it has.

  When a new version of G-WIZ comes as a zip like this one, upload the new
  "Upload to GitHub" files over the old ones.


Using G-WIZ on the iPad
-----------------------
  Touch mode switches on automatically: big controls and a key bar over the
  canvas.
    Undo / Redo   also: tap with two fingers to undo, three fingers to redo
    Shift         straight and 45-degree lines, squares and circles, keep
                  proportions; with the Eraser it ignores edges
    Alt           the Eraser restores; drawing tools pick a color
    Ctrl          with the Move tool, corners distort and edges skew
    2nd           paint, fill and pick with the secondary color; palette taps
                  set the secondary color
    Fit           fit the picture to the screen
    Panels        hide the side panels for a bigger canvas
    Canvas view   hide everything but the picture
  These keys stay on until you tap them again.

  - Canvas view hides the whole interface so only the picture shows. To switch
    it on or off, double-tap the screen with the Pencil tip, tap with four
    fingers, press Tab on a keyboard, or use the "Show interface" button in
    the corner. The tap on the side of the Pencil can't be used: iPadOS only
    passes it to App Store apps, not to web apps. You can turn the Pencil-tip
    double tap off in View > "Pencil double tap toggles canvas view".
  - Tap the Layers title bar to minimize the Layers panel and make room for
    the tool options. Tap it again to bring the panel back.

  - Pinch with two fingers to zoom. Drag with two fingers to move around.
    A finger waits a split second before it paints, so a two- or three-finger
    tap never leaves a mark.
  - Apple Pencil: the first time you use it, G-WIZ makes the Pencil draw and
    fingers pan and zoom, so your hand never leaves marks. Pressure works with
    the brush and pen. Change this in View > "Pencil draws, fingers pan".
  - Press and hold any button to see its name.
  - Open and Import as layer show the Files and Photos picker.
  - Save makes the file, then tap Save... and choose "Save to Files" to pick a
    folder, or "Save Image" to put it in Photos (PNG and GIF). Projects
    (.gwiz) save the same way.
  - An external keyboard works with all the shortcuts (press F1 for the list).

Pixel art
---------
  Tap the green sprite button on the toolbar (or Image > Pixel art mode) and
  tick "Pixel art mode". It works together with Animation mode.
  - Pick a retro screen or sprite size (Game Boy, NES, SNES, Genesis, Game Boy
    Advance, ZX Spectrum, Commodore 64, PICO-8, tiles from 8 x 8 and more) and
    tap "New pixel canvas at this size", or scale the current picture to it.
  - Pick a retro palette (PICO-8, Game Boy, NES, C64, ZX Spectrum, CGA/EGA,
    Apple II, MSX, Master System and others) and a color depth from 1-bit
    (2 colors) to 16-bit. Colors you choose snap to those limits.
  - Reduce colors turns a photo or drawing into pixel art with the palette,
    with or without dithering. The counter turns red when a frame uses more
    colors than allowed.
  - Every tool draws hard pixels. The 1-pixel pencil keeps diagonal lines
    clean, mirror drawing gives left-right, top-bottom or four-way symmetry,
    and the pixel grid and tile grid help line things up.
  - The preview window in the corner shows the real-size picture, with CRT
    scanlines and 4:3 TV stretch if you like. Tap its X to close it.
  - Export scale saves the picture 2 to 8 times larger with crisp pixels.

Animation
---------
  Tap the film-strip button on the toolbar (or Animation > Animation mode)
  and tick "Animation mode". The frame strip then shows every frame.
  - New frame / Duplicate / Delete are on the frame strip and on the
    Animation page, with buttons to move a frame earlier or later. A new
    frame starts empty on the selected layer; other animated layers hold
    their picture. A layer with only one frame stays still on every frame,
    like a background.
  - Onion skin shows faint copies of the frames before (red) and after
    (green). Choose how many frames before and after, how strong they look,
    and whether they show just the selected layer or the whole picture.
    The onion button on the frame strip switches it on and off.
  - Frame length: set how long this frame shows, or a speed for all frames.
  - Motion tween moves, scales, turns or fades the selected layer smoothly
    between two frames. Drag the layer to its start with the Move tool, tap
    "Use the layer's current position" under Start, drag it to its end, tap
    the button under End, pick the frames and easing, then Create tween. A
    dotted path previews the motion. Undo takes it back.
  - Save as GIF to export the animation.

  G-WIZ now starts on a blank white 1280 x 720 picture. Help > Open the
  sample picture brings back the cactus.

New in 1.5
----------
  - Autosave: G-WIZ keeps your picture on the iPad a few seconds after each
    change and whenever you switch away from it. If it was closed with work
    that was not saved as a project, the next start asks "Pick up where you
    left off?". File > Recover autosaved work lists the last three pictures;
    File > Autosave turns it off. Autosaves stay on this iPad only.
    (1.6.1) The bottom of that dialog says whether Safari protects your
    autosaves from being cleared when the iPad runs low on space. Opening
    G-WIZ from its Home Screen icon, rather than in Safari, usually gives this
    protection. Save important work as a project as well.
  - Adding a picture that is bigger than the canvas asks what to do: shrink
    the picture to fit, enlarge the canvas to fit the picture, or keep both
    sizes.
  - Markers are fully opaque by default (lower Opacity or pick Multiply for
    see-through ink).
  - Fixed: the calligraphy pen drew nothing if G-WIZ had been used before.
  - Long menus no longer run off the screen; they switch to two columns.

Removing a background (version 1.4)
-----------------------------------
  Remove background now has two methods. "Smart" (the default) learns the
  background colors from the border, works out what the subject looks like,
  cuts along the picture's real edges and keeps soft edges on hair and fur.
  "Classic" is the old flood from the border.
  - Presets give a starting point: plain background, busy background, hair
    and fur, product photo, logo.
  - Hints: when it guesses wrong, choose "Mark subject" (green) or "Mark
    background" (magenta) and paint over the part, then Remove again. The
    hint brushes are also in the toolbox. "Keep" hints win over everything,
    so a subject that touches the edge of the picture can be rescued with
    one stroke.
  - Sliders: Background tolerance, Stop at edges, Background colors (raise
    it for gradients and busy backgrounds), Sample border width (lower it
    when the subject touches the edge), Refinement passes, "Background must
    connect to the border", Clear enclosed gaps, Edge detail (how far around
    edges hair is studied), Edge softness and Edge contrast.
  - Turn on "Preview before applying" to see the result as red before you
    commit; the preview updates as you move sliders or paint hints.
  - Under the Eraser's Detection settings, "Perceptual color matching" and
    "Ignore fine texture and noise in edges" also apply to the brush, the
    wand and the classic remover.

Drawing (version 1.4)
---------------------
  - Every drawing tool has a Style list: Pencil (hard round, square, grainy,
    sketch), Pen (ink, ballpoint, calligraphy, felt tip, brush pen), Brush
    (soft, hard, bristle, chalk, splatter, watercolor), Marker (chisel, round,
    highlighter, dry) and Airbrush (fine, coarse, soft, spatter, stipple).
  - Pen pressure: a curve editor sets how pressure turns into size and
    opacity (presets or drag the red point), with a smallest size and lowest
    opacity. The green dot shows your last pressure. Pressure now works for
    the marker and airbrush too.
  - Canvas view shows a small tool strip on the left: eraser, restore, the
    drawing tools, bucket and move, a size slider, an opacity slider, the
    primary color and undo/redo. Collapse it with the arrow; View > "Tool
    strip in canvas view" hides it.

  iPad limits
  - Safari limits how much memory a page may use. Pictures are kept to about
    16 megapixels (4000 x 4000), and very long or large animations may not
    fit. Undo keeps fewer steps than on a PC.
  - Safari cannot write WebP files. PNG, GIF and TIFF work.
  - Animated WebP or PNG files may open as a single still frame. Animated GIFs
    open fully.
